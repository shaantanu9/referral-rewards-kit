# Referral kit: compliance re-check against current official text

Updated: 2026-11-20
What this is: every store and legal rule the referral kit relies on (ADR 0001 sections 3 and 8-11), re-read against the
current official text, with the gaps and what to change. Also the limits for eight possible new features.
Related: [growth research](referral-growth.md) · [ADR 0001](../adr/0001-referral-kit.md) · [privacy](../privacy.md) · [store review](../store-review.md)

## How to read this

- **Read date** for every row is 2026-11-20 unless stated otherwise.
- **Quote quality.** "Verbatim" means copied from the official text (PDF extracted locally, or fetched page).
  Text marked *(fetch excerpt)* is an exact excerpt returned by a page-reading tool that only returns short pieces; the words
  are the source's, but the passage may be cut. Text marked *(secondary)* comes from a law firm or tracker, not the
  regulator, because the official page could not be fetched. **[unconfirmed]** = not checked against an official source.
- **Status:** `yes` = the kit as described in the ADR complies · `gap` = change needed · `unknown` = a judgement call
  or not checkable from the text.
- Not legal advice. Several rows are judgement calls; they say so.

## 1. Summary of gaps (rule → what to change)

| # | Rule | Gap | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | India DPDP Act 2023 s.2(f), s.9; DPDP Rules 2025 r.10 | "Child" in India is **under 18**. Verifiable parental consent before processing *any* personal data of a child, and no "tracking or behavioural monitoring of children". Kit default `minimum_age` 13 (D-R9) is too low for India; install tracking and abuse scoring are behavioural monitoring of the user | For Indian users set `minimum_age = 18` (or the app proves verifiable parental consent through its age hook). Make D-R9's default country-aware: 18 for India; the hook must also stop `installs` rows being written for under-age users. In force for Rule 10 on 2027-05-14 (18 months after 2025-11-14 gazette) |
| G2 | DPDP Rules 2025 r.3 (notice), Act s.6 (consent) | DPDP has no "legitimate interest" basis like GDPR; referral data needs consent or s.7(a) "voluntarily provided". Rule 3 notice needs an **itemised** list of data, the purpose, a link to withdraw consent "comparable" in ease, to exercise rights, and to complain to the Board | Turn the friend's claim sentence (4.2 step 4) and a one-time sharer notice into a DPDP-shaped notice for India: itemised data (install id, who invited whom, the friend's activation and purchase status), purpose, withdraw link, rights link, Data Protection Board complaint link. Add a "leave the referral programme" control (withdrawal) |
| G3 | DPDP Rules 2025 r.8(3) (and r.6(1)(e)) | From 2027-05-14 the data fiduciary must keep personal data, traffic data and logs of processing for **at least one year**, "even if X deletes her account" (Rule 8 illustration). Kit purges `events` after 30 days and deletes `installs` and `reward_seen` at account deletion | Make retention per region/config: `events_retention_days` ≥ 365 for India, and keep a minimal processing log (hashed) for one year after deletion. State it in `docs/privacy.md`. Conflicts with GDPR minimisation, so make it a setting, not a global default |
| G4 | Google Play account deletion policy | Play requires a **web link resource** to request account + data deletion, declared in the Data safety form; ADR only covers the in-app path | Integration guide: the app's web deletion page must also call `referral.delete_user`; list the URL in Data safety. Note "Temporary account deactivation … does not qualify" |
| G5 | Google Play Families policy | ADR excludes Apple Kids Category only. Play Families requires "adult action before enabling features that allow children to exchange personal information" and bars persistent identifiers from children | Add: do not use the kit in apps in Play's Families programme or that target children, same as Apple Kids |
| G6 | FTC Endorsement Guides 16 CFR 255.5; ASCI; ICO/BGH context | Disclosure rides only in the share text, which the sharer can edit in WhatsApp etc. The FTC expects the advertiser to take steps so the disclosure appears | Put the disclosure **on the invite page itself** ("<First name> gets a reward if you join and start using <App>"), above the store buttons, in the page language. Keep it in the default share text too |
| G7 | UK ICO PECR guidance; Germany BGH I ZR 208/12 (EU ePrivacy art.13 context); US CAN-SPAM forwarding rule | Incentivising users to send your marketing makes you the "instigator" (UK) / "sender" (US email). Kit is lower risk (system share sheet, user picks recipient, kit never sends), but the prefilled text is the company's and is rewarded | Keep: never collect friends' contact details, never send email/SMS to friends from the server, never an "enter friend's email" field. Make the share text clearly first-person and editable, and say in the integration guide that UK/DE apps carry residual PECR/UWG risk. Record as a known risk, not fixed |
| G8 | ADR line 74-75 | Says "Neither store's text names referral programs explicitly". Apple 5.6.3 names "referrals" | Correct the sentence: Apple 5.6.3 names "referrals to your app" as something not to manipulate; Google Play's pages do not mention referrals |
| G9 | COPPA (16 CFR 312, amended 2025, compliance date 2026-04-22) | Every RPC writes `installs` before the age hook answers; an under-13 user in a general-audience app is "actual knowledge" territory. A persistent identifier is personal information | Check `app_referral_age_ok` before writing `installs` / `codes`; under-age returns `under_age` and writes nothing. State in privacy text that the install id is used only for fraud prevention (internal operations) |
| G10 | EU Digital Fairness Act proposal (COM(2026) 1100, 2026-11-11) Art. 17(1)(c), Annex I Part III | Not law yet (applies 12 months after entry into force). But the claim-window countdown "You can add an invite code for 5 more days" and any future streak/tier/double-week mechanic is the kind of feature the proposal wants switchable | Low priority now. Design new engagement mechanics (tiers, double weeks, reminders) with an off switch per user from day one; keep countdowns only where the deadline is real |
| G11 | US state App Store Accountability Acts (Texas SB 2420 etc.) | ADR 6.2 marks age assurance [unconfirmed]. Texas law is reported in effect after the Fifth Circuit stayed the injunction (2026-05-28) and a 2026-07 Supreme Court order *(secondary)* | Integration guide: where the store passes an age signal, the app's `app_referral_age_ok` should use it. Re-check status before launch [unconfirmed: exact developer duties] |

Everything else in ADR section 3 checks out against the current text (section 2 below).

### 2.4 EU Digital Fairness Act proposal

Official text read: **COM(2026) 1100 final, 2026/0356 (COD), Brussels 11.11.2026**, "Proposal for a REGULATION … on
promoting fair business-to-consumer commercial practices in the offline and digital single market, and repealing
Directive 2005/29/EC (Digital Fairness Act)". PDF:
<https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/0575d59f-554f-4a9e-b54b-e6b717175809_en?filename=Proposal%20for%20a%20Digital%20Fairness%20Act_0.pdf>
(read 2026-11-20). Final article (entry into force and application), paragraph 2: "It shall apply from [12 months after the entry into force]." It is a **proposal**:
Parliament and Council still have to adopt it. Annex I (incl. Part III dark patterns list) was **not** in the PDF read;
the list below is a law firm's summary *(secondary: Pinsent Masons, 2026-11-17,
<https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/digital-fairness-act-addictive-design-dark-patterns-crosshairs>)*.

| Provision | Exact text (verbatim from the proposal unless marked) | Effect on the kit |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 10(1) Dark patterns | "Traders shall not design, organise or operate their online interfaces … in a manner that is likely to deceive or manipulate consumers, or in a manner that is likely to otherwise materially distort the economic behaviour of consumers." | General test for every referral screen |
| Annex I Part III (secondary) | Includes "persistently requesting that consumers make a choice or change their choice", "making false claims about the availability or the popularity of a product", and notifications that "falsely suggest that another consumer, the trader or a third party has contacted the consumer" | Friend reminders must never pretend to come from the inviter; invite prompts must stay rate-limited |
| Art. 11(1) Influencer marketing | "Traders carrying out influencer marketing shall disclose the commercial intent of a communication in a clear, prominent, and unambiguous manner … through the terms ‘advertisement’ or ‘commercial collaboration’." Art. 3(15): "‘influencer marketing’ means a commercial practice whereby traders use their popularity among their audience to directly or indirectly promote products in exchange for payment"; recital 22: payment "encompasses gifts, invitations, and any other payment in kind" | Ordinary friend-to-friend invites: probably not "popularity among their audience" [judgement]. A creator programme (D-R5) or a public leaderboard of top sharers: yes, labels required |
| Art. 12 Personalised advertising | "Traders shall not target advertisements to a consumer based on profiling … (a) by using personal data of the consumer where the trader is aware with reasonable certainty that the consumer is a minor" | Do not use referral data (who invited whom) to profile minors for ads |
| Art. 15 Variable reward systems | "Traders offering or making available to consumers paid-for variable reward systems shall inform consumers about the types of products or virtual items available … and the probability of obtaining them." Art. 3(18) limits it to rewards "acquired through payment transactions" | Free mystery referral rewards are outside Art. 15's letter, but recital 63 explains the gambling concern; disclose odds anyway (section 3) |
| Art. 17(1) Addictive design | Traders whose products "can reasonably be foreseen to encourage compulsive or excessive use, shall make available … effective tools enabling them to disable … (b) notifications not triggered by, or not directly related to, the consumer's interaction or activity on the service; (c) features incentivising engagement at regular times or with greater frequency, including through penalties or loss of benefits for failing to engage regularly or within specified time intervals" | Reward pushes are triggered by the user's own referral activity (fine). Friend reminders, double weeks and tier ladders with expiry are (b)/(c)-style features: give a per-user off switch (G10) |
| Art. 28(1) Unsolicited communications | "Traders shall not use … SMS, MMS or electronic mail for the purposes of direct marketing without the consumer's prior consent within the meaning of Article 4, point (11) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679." | Kit never sends to friends; reminders to a friend who signed up go in-app or by push with opt-in, never email/SMS without consent |
| Streaks / countdowns / referral by name | "streak" and "countdown" do not appear in the operative articles read; "refer a friend" does not appear at all | — |

## 3. New features: compliance limits

| Feature | Rules that bite | Must do | Must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inviter's first name / photo on the friend's invite page** | GDPR (legitimate interest + transparency to the sharer); DPDP r.3 notice (India sharer); Apple 5.1.2(i) consent to share data; FTC 255.5 (the page becomes the endorsement, so the reward must be disclosed next to it) | Opt-in per sharer ("Show my first name and photo on my invite page"), default off; first name only, never surname/email; photo only from the profile the user already set; the page states "<Name> gets a reward if you join" (G6); remove at once on account deletion or code rotation; `noindex` stays | Show it by default; let a code holder enumerate names (rate-limit `/i/CODE` lookups; unknown code shows nothing); show it for under-age users |
| **Reminding a friend who signed up but did not activate** | Apple 4.5.4 (push for promotion needs explicit opt-in + in-app opt-out); India dark patterns "nagging"; DFA Art. 17(1)(b) and Annex I Part III ("falsely suggest that another consumer … has contacted the consumer"); EU/UK ePrivacy/PECR and DFA Art. 28 for email/SMS; CAN-SPAM | In-app card first; push only to users opted in to that notification type; at most 1-2 reminders total, inside `activate_window_days`; message from the app, factual ("Your welcome bonus is waiting: open a chapter to get 30 min"); one-tap "stop these" | Send email/SMS without marketing consent; word it as if the inviter sent it ("Priya is waiting for you!"); remind after the window closes; tell the friend the inviter "will lose" a reward (confirm shaming) |
| **Tiered reward ladder** (e.g. 3 friends → bonus) | FTC 255.5 (bigger incentive = bigger disclosure duty); UK DMCC banned practice 16 / UCPD pyramid (only if the sharer gives consideration); India "bait and switch"; DFA Art. 17(1)(c) if tiers reset or expire | All tiers and caps shown up front and in the terms page; tier counts only rewarded activations (caps still apply); if tiers expire, a per-user off switch (DFA); never require the sharer to pay to take part | Tiers that reset to push engagement ("lose your progress in 3 days"); hide that capped friends don't count; reward tier for installs or reviews |
| **Time-limited double-reward week with countdown** | UK DMCC banned practice 7 and CMA207 countdown example; India "False Urgency" (illustration (b) "falsely creating time-bound pressure"); DFA Annex I Part III (false scarcity); Apple 5.6 (misleading); Play deceptive behavior | Real start and end stored server-side; the countdown ends when stated and the offer really stops; rules (which events count, by when the friend must activate) on the terms page; a friend who claims inside the week gets the double rule even if they activate after it (rule_set_version already does this) | Restart the timer, run back-to-back "last chance" weeks, show per-user fake deadlines, or claim scarcity ("only 50 spots") that is not true |
| **Public referral leaderboard** | Apple 3.2.2(vii) and 5.6.3 (manipulation); DFA Art. 11 + ASCI + FTC (top sharers start to look like influencers, so their posts need labels); GDPR/DPDP (publishing names and counts is new processing, needs opt-in); India nagging/false urgency; prize laws if prizes are attached | Opt-in to appear, pseudonym allowed; counts only rewarded activations after fraud holds; show it in-app only, not on the web; if prizes: official rules, no purchase needed, odds/eligibility, tax handling | Rank by raw installs or claims (invites fraud); show minors; attach cash/large prizes without sweepstakes/prize-competition review; show friends' names |
| **WhatsApp direct-share button with prefilled text** | Apple 5.1.1(iii)/5.1.2(v) (preview, no Contacts); ICO PECR "instigating"; BGH tell-a-friend; FTC 255.5 disclosure; DFA Art. 28 (WhatsApp is not SMS/email, but national ePrivacy rules may treat it as electronic mail) [judgement] | Open WhatsApp's own share (`https://wa.me/?text=` or the share intent) so the user picks the chat and can edit; text written in first person, names the reward, includes the link; preview first | Pre-select a recipient or phone number; read WhatsApp contacts; send via the WhatsApp Business API from the server (that makes the app the sender, consent required) |
| **QR code** | Apple 3.1.1 ("own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as … QR codes") | Encode only the invite **link** (`https://…/i/CODE`); the QR opens the same invite page and the same claim flow; nothing unlocks without server activation | Encode an unlock token or a reward that is granted on scan; let a QR grant premium without IAP |
| **Random / mystery referral rewards** | DFA Art. 15 (paid-for variable rewards: disclose odds; recital 63 gambling concern); EU/UK prize promotion rules; US state lottery law (prize + chance + consideration) and 39 USC 3001(k) model wording; India Online Gaming Act 2025 ("other stakes"); FTC 255.5 (sweepstakes entries are a material connection, staff view *(secondary)*); Apple/Play loot-box odds disclosure **[unconfirmed: current Apple/Play loot-box clauses not re-read]** | Every outcome and its odds shown before the share; every outcome is a real reward (no "nothing"); outcomes are in-app time only, no cash value; nobody pays for or speeds up a draw; off for minors | Sell extra spins or let paid credits buy them; cash, vouchers or transferable prizes without a sweepstakes/prize-competition review; hide odds; offer to under-18s in India/EU |
